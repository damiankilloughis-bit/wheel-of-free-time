# Handstand: Lesson 3: Peel your heels off the wall

**▶️ Watch first:** [Back to Wall Handstand Balance Drills: USE These 3 Wall Techniques](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=stVj9X6RRFk&t=80s) (Paul Twyman, 7:33). Start at **1:20** for the fingertip peel and why your body has to stay "one piece," then **2:20** for the shoulder method.

**Status (Oct 16):** toe pulls on the chest-to-wall are ✅ solved. Back-to-wall, your heels won't leave the wall. Heel-of-palm presses and pushing your hips over your hands didn't help. On the floor you only ever kick into the under-committed side.

---

## Why you're stuck

**1. You're pressing the button that pushes you *into* the wall.**
- Back-to-wall with your heels on it means you're leaning toward the **overbalance** side (onto your back), and the wall is catching you. Twyman calls the heel pull "controlling the over balance… when I go towards my back" (Twyman 3-techniques **1:20**, fingertip video **0:00**).
- Pressing the **heel of your palm** moves you *toward* the wall. Shana uses it to get "on and stuck to the wall" ([Meyerson **11:40**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9tNHOXOzMRE&t=700s)). Pushing your hips over your hands does the same thing.
- What gets your heels off is your **fingertips** (or moving your shoulders, see Drill C).
- 🔎 **Self-check:** press the heel of your palm. Do your heels feel *heavier* on the wall? Then that's the wrong button for this drill.

**2. Your body isn't one piece, so the push never reaches your heels.**
- Twyman: "the biggest mistake most people make… they don't keep the body as one segment… we bend the arms or we change the position… we whip the feet… collapse back towards the wall" ([**0:40–1:00**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QP15ioROXvk&t=40s)).
- Antranik: squeeze your glutes, push up through your shoulders, point your toes. "Your feet are gonna come off the wall **only** from you gripping the floor with your fingers." **Don't** pull your feet away with your leg muscles ([**0:40–1:20**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q6Xcsw0O23I&t=40s)).
- 🔎 **Self-check:** film from the side while you press your fingers. If anything bends (elbows, lower back, legs splitting), the energy is leaking out there.

**3. Your hands are the wrong distance from the wall.**
- Antranik: "don't do it too far, otherwise it's going to be very hard to have the feet come off the wall, and if you do it too close you're always going to come down" (**2:20**).
- The further away your hands are, the more your body curves to reach the wall (Twyman **2:40–3:00**). Shana says having your whole foot on the wall with your chest pushed out is a shape you can't hold ([**3:00**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9tNHOXOzMRE&t=180s)).
- 🔎 **Self-check:** in the side video, is your body a curve, with your lower back arched and your heels reaching for the wall? If so, move your hands closer.

**On the floor (always under-committing):** Twyman says most people "keep their body behind… putting the safety brake on" so they don't fall on their back ([**5:00**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JyyKGE-Ajs8&t=300s)). That's your floor problem (see Drill D).

## Techniques → drills

**A. Make it one piece first** (standing at the wall)
- Turn around and put your forehead on the wall. Push through your toes to pull yourself off the wall. Your fingers do exactly that job in the handstand (fingertip video **0:20**).
- **5 reps**, slow. ✅ Working if you come off the wall smoothly without bending at the hips.

**B. Fingertip "flutters," crossed legs** (back to wall, hands on a tape mark)
- Put tape where your index fingers go, starting **closer than usual** (Twyman uses a marker, **2:40**). Use a hard floor, not carpet or grass (Antranik **2:00**).
- Kick up, **cross your legs** ("much easier to come off the wall cuz your body's acting as that one piece," fingertip video **0:40**), squeeze your glutes, push tall, and lock your arms.
- Grip with your fingers until your heels *barely* leave the wall, then relax and go back. Twyman's "flutters" are like a car on a hill "with just enough on the accelerator" (**1:20**).
- **2 sets × 5 reps.** Antranik's rule: if your feet drop to the floor, add 2 reps (**3:40**).
- ✅ Working if your heels leave the wall without your arms or hips moving, even by half an inch.
- ❌ If nothing moves, move the tape **closer** by a couple of inches. If you keep falling off toward your feet, move it a bit **further**.

**C. Shoulder peel** (back to wall, hands a little further out than in B)
- Kick up and kiss the wall. Move your **shoulders further away from the wall** until "there's not enough weight the other side of my body" and your heels come off.
- **The instant they leave, push up tall** to catch the handstand. Otherwise you fall "this way too fast" (Twyman 3-techniques **2:20–3:40**).
- **4 reps.** ✅ Working if your heels come off and you *catch* (even 1 s) instead of dropping to your feet.

**D. Release the safety brake** (floor, away from the wall)
- Kick slowly into a slight banana with your shoulders forward, catch it, then push out toward straight "as much as you feel like you're balanced" (Twyman freestanding **6:40–8:20**). It's safe because most of your weight is still on the side where you'd come down onto your feet (**5:40**).
- **5 kick-ups.** ✅ Working if your legs actually reach vertical (or past it) before you come down, instead of stopping short.

## The 15 minutes

- **0:00–1:30** · Wrists + **Drill A** (5 forehead-to-wall toe pulls)
- **1:30–2:30** · Put down tape, 1 test kick-up, and prop your phone for a side view
- **2:30–6:30** · **Drill B**: 2 × 5 fingertip flutters, legs crossed, rest 30 s between sets. Adjust the tape between sets.
- **6:30–9:30** · **Drill C**: 4 shoulder peels. Push up the moment your heels leave.
- **9:30–10:30** · Back to **Drill B** at your best tape distance: 3 reps, holding each off the wall for a count of 2–3
- **10:30–13:30** · **Drill D**: 5 slow banana kick-ups on the floor
- **13:30–15:00** · **Test:** 3 heel pulls. Log your best number of seconds off the wall and which tape distance worked.

## What the people who struggled said
- **jackhwoods** spent years on drills and still couldn't show "a reliable handstand." His advice: *"the more we practice bad handstands, the better we get at doing bad handstands"* ([**9:00**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xA5Fx4x9-O4&t=540s)). Stop when your shape breaks down.
- **Paul Twyman** started at 37: *"learning to balance is very tricky, especially as an adult."* This drill alone "will keep people busy for around six months, nine months" ([**2:20**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QP15ioROXvk&t=140s)). Being stuck here is normal.
- **SUBYHANDSTANDS:** fear of falling is "probably one of the most common things stopping adults." If you don't face it, "you're always going to be stuck with having the wall behind you" ([**4:00**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m0fXjgL9_BU&t=240s)).
- **Antranik:** a still handstand isn't still. People are "constantly doing micro adjustments," and this drill teaches you "how important the hands are" (**1:40**).

## Target for next time
- **5 heel pulls in a row**, with your heels clearly off and nothing bending.
- **One hold of 5 s** off the wall. Twyman's bar before freestanding is 5–10 reps plus a 10–15 s hold ([**2:00**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JyyKGE-Ajs8&t=120s)).
- Write down your best tape distance.
- Floor: at least **2 of 5** kick-ups reach vertical.
