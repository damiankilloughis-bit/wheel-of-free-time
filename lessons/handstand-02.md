# Handstand: Lesson 2: Stop falling back (weight shift & balance control)

**Your problem:** underbalance. Your feet fall back toward the kick side because your hips never get all the way over your hands.
**Today's goal:** get your hips stacked over your shoulders and hands, and learn which part of your hand moves you which way.

---

## The 15 minutes

**0:00–1:30 · Wrists + "earmuffs"** (standing)
- Circle your wrists, then do palm rocks on the floor for 30 s.
- Stand with your arms overhead. Tuck your pelvis ("tip the soup bowl out the back") and push the sky up with your shoulders at your ears. Hold 2 × 15 s.
- ✅ Working if your ribs/belly stay in and your arms touch your ears.

**1:30–3:30 · Chest-to-wall hold** (wall)
- Walk up the wall facing it and walk your hands in close. Only your toes touch the wall.
- Hold 2 × 20–30 s. Rest 20 s between holds.
- ✅ Working if your toes feel lighter each set (more weight in your hands) and your belly isn't on the wall.

**3:30–6:30 · Fingertips-at-wall kick-ups** (wall)
- Put your fingertips touching the wall. Not an inch away, *touching*.
- Drop your head and take a big split. Make it a **light** kick aimed at the **ceiling**, not the wall.
- Once up, **press into the heel of your palm** to stick to the wall, and get your back flat on the wall.
- Do 5 tries, about 30 s each including rest.
- ✅ Working if you stop bouncing off and stick stacked (hips over shoulders over wrists). Ricocheting off at first is normal.

**6:30–9:30 · Pull-offs, both feet together** (back to wall: lesson 1, level 2)
- Kick up and squeeze your legs. Push your fingers to pull your body off the wall.
- Do 6 reps. Hold each one 2–3 s, then come back to the wall.
- Both feet leave at the same time. Don't push off with a toe.
- ✅ Working if your feet stay over you while you're off the wall instead of drifting back to the kick side.

**9:30–12:00 · Underbalance save** (chest to wall, tiny range)
- Stand a bit away from the wall in a dish shape, wearing socks.
- Slowly move your **shoulders forward** so your toes slide a few inches down the wall, then push back up.
- Do 4–5 slow reps.
- ✅ Working if more weight goes into your hands and your toes get *light*. If you jerk back into the wall, that's fear: make the range smaller.
- (Twyman calls this an advanced drill. Today is just *playing* with a small range.)

**12:00–15:00 · Filmed kick-ups** (away from wall)
- Prop your phone to film you **from the side**. Do 4–6 kick-ups.
- Before each one, set your hands shoulder-width with your index fingers forward and your fingers spread.
- Check: are your **shoulders on top of your hands** the whole time? Do your hips get over your shoulders, or do your legs reach over while your hips hang back?
- **Stop as soon as your form gets sloppy.**
- ✅ Log your best hold in seconds.

---

## Quick tips → which drill they come from

- **Hips, not legs.** If your hips don't get high enough, your legs reach over to compensate and that *pulls you down to the floor*. That's your exact problem. → Drills: fingertips-at-wall, filmed kick-ups.
- **Heel of palm vs. fingers.** Pressing the **heel of your palm** moves you *toward your fingers*. Pressing your **fingertips** pushes you *back toward your heels*. So when you're falling back, ease off your fingers, press the heel of your palm, and move your shoulders forward. → Drills: fingertips-at-wall, underbalance save.
- **Shoulders stay on top of your hands.** Don't let your shoulders slide around to get under your hips. → Drill: filmed kick-ups.
- **Your base does the balancing,** like feet and ankles do when you stand: fingers, a little elbow, a little shoulder. If your hips do the work, you go top-heavy. → All drills.
- **Kick light, aim up.** A hard kick means you bounce off. A bigger split means a slower kick, and a slower kick means more control. → Drills: fingertips-at-wall, filmed kick-ups.
- **Grow your "window."** The goal isn't to never be under- or overbalanced. It's to be able to drift either way and come back. Twyman says to spend *more* time slightly underbalanced, like sitting back on your heels. → Drills: pull-offs, underbalance save.
- **Quality over volume.** Tired, sloppy reps train sloppy handstands. Do a few minutes often, ideally daily. → Built into the 15 minutes.

## What the people who struggled said

**Paul Twyman** started handstands at 37, has practiced daily for 10–11 years, and is now 49.
*"Learning to balance is very tricky, especially as an adult, and we need to convince your body to do it."* (11:00)
*"We don't want the hips to do the work because that's going to make us topheavy."* (8:20)
*"If you're scared, you're going to do this, which is going to put more weight back into the wall. We want to do the opposite… the shoulders are controlling the movement."* (6:20–6:40)

**jackhwoods** spent *"years on a long list of drills and exercises that left me still unable to show anyone a reliable handstand."* (0:00)
*"Where most people go wrong is thinking that they can outskill their lack of strength. I made this mistake for years and it just drove me insane."* (1:40)
*"The more we practice bad handstands, the better we get at doing bad handstands."* (9:00). So stop as soon as your form breaks (9:40).
Your goal is *"less and less weight on your toes… until there's no load on them at all."* (8:20)
(He says to skip back-to-wall work completely. Twyman uses it. This lesson does mostly chest-to-wall work and keeps one short back-to-wall block.)

**Shana Meyerson** on why people fall:
*"If my hips don't go high enough and my leg tries to compensate… it's gonna pull me down to the floor."* (2:20)
*"When I press into the heel of the palm it pushes me this way; when I press into my fingertips it pushes me that way."* (11:40)
*"Remember, aim for the sky."* (8:40)
On the fingertips-at-wall drill: bouncing off is *"normal, do not give up on this one."* (7:20)
